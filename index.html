The engine

Nine AI opponents, one real engine

Choose an AI level from Beginner to Maximum when you play. Analysis is a separate board for inspecting positions after the game or exploring your own variations.

  • No level throws the game. Every level from 1 to 9 takes an immediate win when one is on the board, and blocks one when it is not.
  • Two workers, no freezing. The move search and the position analysis run on separate background threads, so the page stays responsive while the engine thinks.
  • Stop whenever you want. A search on the infinite budget runs until you end it.

Analysis

See the position the way the engine sees it

Scores are always shown from X’s point of view: positive means X is better, negative means O is better. One convention, everywhere, so a number never means two different things.

  • Evaluation bar and graph. A bar beside the board and a line through the whole game, both updating as each search depth completes.
  • Candidates and principal variation. The shortlist the engine considered, ranked, plus the line it expects to follow.
  • Move grading. Each move is labelled Best, Good, Inaccuracy or Blunder from the swing in evaluation it caused.
  • Proven mates. Forced wins are shown as M1, M2… only once the tactical solver has verified them.

How to play

If you have played tic-tac-toe you already know most of it. The board is just bigger, and you need five.

  1. X always moves first. Players alternate, placing one mark on any empty square.
  2. Five in a row wins — horizontally, vertically or diagonally.
  3. Watch the threats. An unblocked line of three or four is usually the whole game.
  4. A full board with no five is a draw, though on 225 squares that almost never happens.

About XO 5-in-a-Row

What this is

XO 5-in-a-Row is five-in-a-row played on a 15×15 board. X and O take turns placing a mark on any empty square, X always moves first, and the first player to line up five in a row — across, down or diagonally — wins. A full board with nobody on five is a draw.

It began as a search engine written for the game, and the interface came afterwards to give the engine somewhere to play. Everything runs in your browser: the engine, the analysis and your saved games never leave the device.

Ways to play

  • Play vs AI — nine engine levels, playing as either X or O.
  • Local 2 player — two people on one device, with the engine out of the way.
  • Analysis board — place stones for both sides, branch into variations, and search any position.
  • Puzzles — solver-verified positions where X has a forced win. Your answer is re-checked by the engine rather than matched against a stored one.

Time controls

1s, 5s, 30s, 60s, or an infinite search you end yourself with Stop.

Two workers

Move search and position analysis run on separate background threads, so neither blocks the page.

How to read the numbers

Scores are always from X’s point of view: positive means X is better, negative means O is better, zero is level. That one convention holds in the evaluation bar, the evaluation number, the graph, the candidate list and the end-of-game summary, so a number never changes meaning depending on whose turn it is.

How it is built

  • A single self-contained HTML page — no framework, no build step at runtime, no network requests once it has loaded.
  • Alpha-beta search with iterative deepening, a transposition table and a separate VCF/VCT solver for forced wins.
  • Statistics, achievements, settings and saved games live in this browser’s local storage. There are no accounts and nothing is uploaded.

Development status

This is a personal project under active development. Features, engine behaviour and interface details are still changing, and some parts are experimental. The multi-line analysis shows a ranked candidate list rather than independently searched lines, the variation tree is a flat list, and the puzzle bank is a small fixed set. Nothing shown anywhere in this app is a mock-up of a feature that does not exist.
